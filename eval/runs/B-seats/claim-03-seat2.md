# Seat 2 — Data & inference validity

*Tier read: 2 (single decision document, three seats). Per the skill's own Application-strength note, I am tuning one default and saying so: this section exceeds the tier-2 per-member length ceiling. Three separate critical findings are anchored below and cutting any of them to hit 250 words would drop a finding that changes the decision. No other default dropped.*

**Role & remit.** Financial-analysis reviewer for build-vs-buy cases: I judge the inputs, the stated and unstated assumptions, what has been excluded, and whether the recommendation follows from the figures shown. Standard applied, offered as my professional judgment rather than a named external convention: a memo recommending irreversible contract termination must (a) compare against the correct counterfactual, (b) carry its own stated one-time transition cost into any payback claim, (c) disclose exclusions symmetrically on both sides, and (d) show sensitivity on its largest uncertain input. Arithmetic is seat 1's remit; where a figure error is visible from the inputs I report it and mark the overlap.

**Assessment.** Unit rates are disclosed and three of five cost lines are re-derivable, which is better than most memos of this length. But the comparison is built against the wrong baseline, omits the one-time cost the memo itself states, and understates the self-hosted side through two figure errors that both run in the same direction. Recomputed on the artifact's own inputs and its own annualization method, the saving falls from $71,260 (34%) to roughly $47,140 (22%), and one plausible correction to a single unsourced assumption reverses the recommendation outright. The conclusion does not follow from the figures shown.

**Strengths.** The $18,000 overage exclusion is disclosed with a stated reason rather than dropped silently — "which we have excluded here because the new contract caps it" (§Current spend). Compute, storage, and egress each state quantity and unit rate (§Proposed self-hosted setup), so a reader can audit them without trusting the author.

**Weaknesses, risks & errors.**

1. **Critical, defect — the recommendation rests on one unsourced assumption that can flip it.** Anchor: "We would need half an engineer to run it, costed at a fully loaded $180,000 FTE" (§Proposed self-hosted setup). No basis is given for 0.5, and at $90,000 it is the largest line in the table — 65% of the stated total. At 1.0 FTE the self-hosted total exceeds Streamvault's $210,000 and the saving becomes a loss. No sensitivity or scenario analysis appears anywhere in the memo.

2. **Critical, defect — "payback is immediate" contradicts a cost the memo states three paragraphs earlier.** Anchors: "Migration itself is roughly four months of engineering time" (§Proposed self-hosted setup) and "Payback is immediate because there is no capital outlay on reserved instances beyond the first invoice" (§Conclusion). The migration cost never enters any figure. At the memo's own $180,000 FTE, four engineer-months is ~$60,000; payback is then over a year, not immediate. The stated reason is also a non sequitur — absence of capital outlay is a cash-flow fact, not a payback calculation. Compounding this, "four months of engineering time" does not state headcount, so it cannot be costed as written: one engineer for four months and three engineers for four calendar months differ threefold.

3. **Critical, defect — the recommended timeline leaves an uncovered gap.** Anchors: "renews 1 December 2026" (§Current spend) and "giving notice on the Streamvault contract before the 1 October renewal deadline and starting the migration in Q4" (§Conclusion). A migration starting no earlier than 1 October and running four months completes around February; the contract ends 1 December. A recipient acting on this as written is either without an ingestion platform for roughly two months or paying twice — neither is costed. Two different dates are also both attached to renewal (1 October, 1 December) with no notice period stated. Overlaps the decision red-team seat; reported because it is visible from the figures.

4. **Major, defect — the baseline is the wrong counterfactual.** Anchor: "Streamvault invoices us $210,000 per year on the current contract, which renews 1 December 2026" (§Current spend). The decision is whether to sign the renewal, so the comparison should be against the renewal price, not the current one. The memo demonstrably knows the new contract's terms — it cites the overage cap — yet never states its price. Whether the saving is 22% or zero depends on a number the author appears to hold and did not show.

5. **Major, defect — two figure errors, both understating the self-hosted total.** Anchors: "| Storage (40 TB, gp3) | $920 |" and "| Monitoring and tooling | $14,000 |" against "| **Total** | **$138,740** |" (§Annual cost). At the memo's own rate, 40 TB × $0.023/GB-month is $920 *per month*, i.e. ~$11,040 a year — the egress line from the same sentence was annualized, the storage line was not. Separately, the listed lines sum to $152,740; the stated total omits the $14,000 monitoring line entirely. Corrected: ~$162,860. Arithmetic is seat 1's remit; flagged here because both errors propagate into the $71,260 and 34% claims.

6. **Major, defect — exclusions are disclosed asymmetrically, and the one disclosed exclusion is not justified by its own reason.** Anchor: "Volume-based overage in the last two quarters added a further $18,000, which we have excluded here because the new contract caps it" (§Current spend). A cap is a ceiling, not zero, and the cap value is never given, so full exclusion does not follow. Note the pattern: this is the only exclusion disclosed and the only one favouring the incumbent, while the larger undisclosed exclusions (migration, monitoring, redundancy) all favour self-hosting.

7. **Major, defect — the two sides are not like-for-like.** Anchor: the cost table's line set, ending "| **Total** | **$138,740** |" (§Annual cost). Six instances of raw infrastructure plus half an engineer are compared against a managed vendor service. Rows a competent reviewer would expect and cannot find: backup and disaster recovery, redundancy beyond the six instances, out-of-hours or on-call cover, cloud support plan, and any SLA-equivalent. Whatever Streamvault's $210,000 includes beyond raw capacity is priced at zero on the self-hosted side.

8. **Minor, defect — precision claimed exceeds precision of inputs.** Anchor: "a saving of **$71,260** a year, or 34%" (§Conclusion), derived from inputs described as "roughly four months" and "about 18 TB a month". Dollar-exact savings from admittedly approximate inputs overstate the certainty of the result.

*Not a defect, recorded so it is not mistaken for one:* stating the recommendation in the header before the evidence is normal memo practice, not bias in itself — my concern in item 6 is the direction of the exclusions, not the placement of the conclusion.

**Gaps.** No sensitivity or break-even analysis on the 0.5 FTE or the migration estimate. No renewal quote. No multi-year view, despite the plan committing to three-year reserved pricing while presenting a one-year comparison. No growth assumption: storage is held flat at 40 TB and egress flat at 18 TB/month across the commitment, even though the memo's own evidence — volume-based overage in two consecutive quarters — indicates volume is variable and rising. Volume risk is acknowledged on the vendor side and modelled as zero on the self-hosted side. The $14,000 monitoring line is the only figure with no derivation at all. The memo also evaluates exactly two options; it can show self-hosting beats today's Streamvault price, not that it beats renegotiation or an alternative vendor (overlaps the decision red-team seat).

**Strongest reason this might be fundamentally wrong.** Not that the saving is overstated, but that its sign is unestablished. The self-hosted side is understated by two figure errors and by omissions that all push one way, while the vendor side is compared against a price that is not the one on offer. Correcting only what is derivable from the artifact's own text takes the saving to ~22%; adding an unpriced migration, DR and on-call cover, or a staffing estimate of 1.0 rather than 0.5 FTE consumes the remainder. If that is right, the 34% figure is a product of how the model was assembled rather than a finding about the two options, and the memo's central recommendation may be backwards.

**Domain verdict.** Below the bar for a decision of this size. A competent analyst producing a memo that triggers an irreversible contract termination would state the counterfactual price, carry the one-time migration cost into the payback claim, and show a break-even on the largest soft assumption. This memo does none of the three, and its headline figure does not survive recomputation on its own inputs.

**Recommended fixes.**
1. Rebuild the table against the *renewal* quote, not the current invoice; if the renewal price is not yet available, state that and mark the comparison provisional.
2. Fix the storage line to an annual figure (~$11,040) and include the $14,000 monitoring line in the total; republish the corrected total, saving, and percentage.
3. Add a one-time migration cost with headcount stated, and replace "payback is immediate" with a computed payback period in months.
4. Show a break-even on the operations assumption: state the FTE level at which self-hosting stops saving money, and the evidence for choosing 0.5.
5. Add rows for backup/DR, redundancy, on-call cover, and support plan, or state explicitly that the self-hosted option accepts a lower service level than Streamvault provides and have the reviewer accept that trade knowingly.
6. Reconcile the timeline: state the notice period, and show the migration completing before 1 December or cost the overlap.
7. Model at least one growth scenario for storage and egress across the three-year reserved commitment.
