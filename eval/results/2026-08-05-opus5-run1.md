### Precision, and why the raw number misleads

Scored by "is each reported finding defensible on inspection", precision is roughly 99%. That number should not be quoted without the qualification that follows.

Almost every unplanted finding is *true*. Very few are *load-bearing*. Arm A reported 10 majors on the privacy notice and 7 on the churn memo, and the bulk of the unplanted ones are completeness observations: no international transfer disclosure, no error rate reported, no limitations section, no data-quality notes. Each is a real gap. Together they are a wall a recipient has to triage before acting, which is the failure mode the skill's length budget and its "cut the least load-bearing" rule exist to prevent.

The honest reading is that the control arm has high recall and high defensibility and poor prioritisation. Precision as defined in `scoring.md` does not capture that, which is a defect in the measure, recorded here for the next revision.

## Contamination found during the run

Three of my own errors, all surfaced by the reviews rather than by me.

**The ground truth was miscounted.** `code-01` was headed 3 major and its table lists 2. Real totals are 89 planted defects, 62 critical or major. Corrected in `ground-truth.md`, `results/template.md` and the eval README.

**Two of the three probes carry real critical-or-major defects.** They were built to carry none, so the manufactured-fault gate cannot be read cleanly on this run.

- `doc-03` on-call runbook: `$BROKERS` and `$OS_ENDPOINT` are used but never defined, in a runbook that states it assumes no prior knowledge of the indexer. Step 2 tells the operator to look for a "growing" `rejected` count from a single sample of a counter that is cumulative since node start. The "known false positive" section describes a 2-minute artifact that cannot satisfy the alert's own 10-consecutive-minute firing condition. Six unplanted findings, all judged real.
- `claim-05` capacity forecast: "a third large migration would cross it" is wrong — the gap to the threshold is 330 TB and the two contracted migrations are 105 TB each. The deletion-sensitivity figure of 1.95 PB is asserted, not derived, and recomputes closer to 1.85 PB. Both introduced by the author while correcting the scenario arithmetic earlier the same session.

**Two defect-carrying artifacts contain unplanted arithmetic errors.** `claim-01` states p = 0.0038; the reported counts give z = 3.149 and p ≈ 0.0017. `claim-06` claims 40% adoption cuts blended churn to 9.6%; on the memo's own rates it gives 0.40 × 4.1 + 0.60 × 17.3 = 12.0%, and the stated current blend of 14.8% recomputes to 14.5%.

The artifacts were **not** corrected mid-run. Arms A and C had already executed against them, and changing the inputs would have made arm B incomparable. They are fixed for run 2; run 1 is reported with the contamination stated.

### Where the council genuinely won

Two places, and they are real.

**It caught defects arm A missed.** On the job posting, arm C found the stacked-hard-requirements defect that arm A did not. On the privacy notice it found the uploaded-content retention gap. On the churn memo it named the survivorship problem specifically, where arm A only got near it.

**It withdraws.** The verification pass removed findings in 14 of 18 runs, 1 to 3 each. Nothing in arm A can do that, and the withdrawals were, on inspection, correct. On the privacy notice arm C reported 10 findings against arm A's 16 and lost no planted defect doing it — same critical, tighter list. That is the prioritisation problem arm A has, partly solved.

### Where it lost

**It manufactured faults on the clean probe.** `code-03` is the only probe with no real critical or major defect in it. Arm A reported zero. Arm C reported **four majors**, of which at most one is defensible at that severity:

- *Public mutable capacity* — real, but it is a planted **minor**. Inflation.
- *"No per-key invalidation or TTL; hot keys served stale indefinitely"* — the docstring promises an LRU cache. TTL was never offered. A feature request tagged as a defect, which non-negotiable 5 forbids by name.
- *"No eviction counter... capacity unmeasurable"* — an observability wish, not a major defect.
- *"Instance lifetime ambiguous"* — the docstring says instances are per request handler and never shared across threads. That is not ambiguous.

**Severity inflation is worse than arm A's, not better.** Arm A rated 39 findings critical against 21 planted. Arm C rated more on several artifacts: 5 criticals on the cost model against 2 planted, 3 on the retry helper against 0, 3 on the migration plan against 3 but with different ones elevated. The verification pass tightens the *list* and does nothing to calibrate the *scale*.

**It cannot hold the length budget.** Every completed arm C review overran. Measured against the tier ceilings this morning's revision set: 3,192 words against 1,800 on the invoice, 3,848 against 1,800 on the job posting, 4,660 against 3,000 on the cost model, 5,563 against 3,000 on the A/B readout. Mean 3,630 words. Not one run came in under budget.

To the skill's credit, they all disclosed it rather than truncating, and several cited the application-strength note designating the numbers as tunable. The disclosure machinery works. The numbers are simply wrong, and they are wrong by roughly a factor of two.

### Verdict against the pre-registered rule

> Recall +≥15pp — **failed** (+5.7)
> No precision lost — **arguable**; the finding list is tighter, the severity scale is looser
> Zero manufactured faults on probes — **failed** (4 majors on the one clean probe)

**On this evidence the sequential council does not earn its cost.** Two of three conditions failed outright and the recall gain is a third of what was required. Anyone whose reviewer cannot run isolated seats should use the quick check or a single careful pass, and should not pay 2.3× for the ceremony.

This is the arm the skill itself calls the weaker configuration, so it is not a verdict on the skill's default. It is a verdict on the fallback, and the fallback is what most users without subagent tooling will actually get.

## Arm B — isolated council: the manufactured-fault result

**Isolation plus a verification pass largely fixes arm C's worst failure.**

`code-03` is the only genuinely clean probe. Four conditions, same artifact:

| B — isolated, nudged prompt | 0 | **0** | 3 | 5 | approve with minor revisions |
| C — sequential council | 0 | **4** | 5 | 1 | revise substantially before use |

Arm C reported four majors, of which two to three were not defects at all — a TTL feature request against a cache whose docstring never offered TTL, and observability wishes. Arm B's clean run reported **one**, and it is a real defect (the public `capacity` knob) carried at inflated severity rather than a fabrication. Every manufactured finding was withdrawn.

The three isolated seats individually produced the *same* over-reports arm C did — seat 2 raised the identical TTL finding. So isolation does not make seats more restrained. **The verification pass is what removes the fabrications**, and it can only do that when it runs somewhere that did not produce them.

Both executives independently diagnosed the same root cause for the seats' convergence, which is the behaviour non-negotiable 3 specifies:

> the three seats converged because all three sourced the same unwarranted premise from the `# util/lru.py` filename comment — that this is a shared service cache — which the docstring denies two lines later

That is testing why seats agree, locating the shared assumption, and attacking the assumption rather than the agreement. The clean run went further and verified disputed behavioural claims **by executing the artifact**, which the skill does not ask for.

### The clearest demonstration of what the verification pass is for

On `claim-03`, all three isolated seats independently produced a "two-month service gap" finding resting on a shared unstated assumption: that "roughly four months of engineering time" means four *calendar* months. The artifact never says that, and each seat separately flagged the phrase as ambiguous elsewhere in its own analysis without noticing it had relied on one reading.

Three seats that could not see each other converged on the same unexamined premise. The verifier caught it and **corrected rather than withdrew** — what survives is the sequencing defect, which holds under either reading. It also withdrew two fabrications outright: a costed vendor data-export charge for data the artifact never says the vendor holds, and a "bus factor 0.5" claim that read a headcount out of a capacity figure.

This is the sharpest evidence in the run for the skill's central design bet, and it is not the bet as stated. Independence did not stop the seats sharing an assumption. What caught it was a verifier positioned outside all three, doing exactly what non-negotiable 3 prescribes: asking why they agree and attacking the assumption rather than the agreement.

On `doc-06`, arm B found 4/4 planted defects, matching arm C and beating arm A's 3/4, and withdrew 2 while correcting 5.

### What this changes

The arm C verdict stands for arm C. It does not transfer to the default path.

- **Recall** is roughly equal across B and C on the artifacts measured. Isolation does not find more.
- **Precision and calibration** are where isolation pays. The gate failure that sank arm C does not reproduce under arm B.
- The skill's own claim — that a seat which can see another's verdict is not an independent reading — is supported here in a narrower form than stated: the seats behave much the same either way, but a verifier that shares their context inherits their errors and a verifier that does not, catches them.

**Caveat, stated plainly: three artifacts.** This indicates, it does not settle. Arm B needs the full 18 before any of the above is quotable as a measurement.

### A methodology error I made, recorded

The first `code-03` executive prompt contained an instruction the other two executives did not receive: "Apply non-negotiable 5 strictly... A capability the artifact never claimed is not a defect." That text is drawn from the skill, but surfacing it in the prompt is a nudge the default path does not supply, and I did it on the one artifact that decides the manufactured-fault question.

Both runs are kept. The clean replicate is the arm B measurement. The nudged run is a separate and useful condition: it shows a prompt-level reminder takes the surviving major to zero, which suggests the skill's non-negotiable 5 is under-weighted where the executive actually reads it.

## Arm B — remaining coverage

Running as a three-artifact spot check (`code-03`, `doc-06`, `claim-03`), not a full arm. Scoping decision made on budget, stated rather than hidden: a three-artifact result cannot settle the isolation question, only indicate.

The first orchestration design failed and is worth recording. Nested background subagents return into a notification stream their parent has already exited, so the seat text never reached the executive. The orchestrator handled this correctly — it reported that it did not hold the seat analyses and refused to reconstruct them or review the artifact itself. The redesign has each seat write to its own file and the executive read the three files, which removes the context handoff entirely.

The orphaned seats from the failed design are worth keeping as evidence: they were markedly better than either arm's internal seats, carrying `[unverified — recall, not lookup]` tags on recalled conventions and volunteering caveats against their own findings ("if notification and comms are tracked in a separate ticket, both collapse to minor"). Whether that quality survives into the executive's output is exactly what arm B has to show.

## What I would change about this eval

1. **Precision needs a second measure.** Defensibility and prioritisation are different things and only the first is captured. Add a "top-3 agreement" score: does the reviewer's own top three findings match the three a competent owner would act on first?
2. **Grade blind.** Achievable by having a separate pass strip condition labels before grading.
3. **The probes need to be verified, not merely intended.** Two of three were wrong. Before run 2, every probe gets an independent adversarial pass whose only job is to find a critical or major defect in it, and it ships as a probe only if that pass comes back empty.
4. **The author wrote both the artifacts and the ground truth and is grading the outputs.** The recall figure inherits every blind spot in that one head. An independent defect-planter would make this materially stronger.
