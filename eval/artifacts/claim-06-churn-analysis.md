# Why customers stay: the Workflows effect

**Prepared by:** customer analytics
**Date:** 12 July 2026
**Ask:** approve moving two engineers onto Workflows adoption for H2.

## Question

What distinguishes customers who stay from customers who leave?

## Data

Every account record in the CRM as of 1 July 2026 — 8,412 accounts. For each we pulled tenure, seat count, segment, monthly active usage, and which product modules the account has enabled.

The CRM archives and purges account records twelve months after closure, so the extract reflects accounts open today plus those closed within the last year.

## Finding

Accounts with the Workflows module enabled churn at **4.1%** annually. Accounts without it churn at **17.3%**. Workflows users are more than four times less likely to leave.

The gap holds across tenure bands:

| Tenure | Churn, Workflows enabled | Churn, not enabled |
|---|---|---|
| <1 year | 6.2% | 21.4% |
| 1–3 years | 3.9% | 15.1% |
| 3+ years | 2.4% | 9.8% |

## Adoption by segment

| Segment | Accounts | Workflows enabled |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise (>500 seats) | 505 | 81% |
| Mid-market (50–500 seats) | 1,851 | 44% |
| Small (<50 seats) | 6,056 | 9% |

Workflows requires the SSO integration, which is available on Business and Enterprise plans only.

## Conclusion

Workflows is the strongest retention lever in the product. Driving adoption from the current 21% to 40% would cut blended churn from 14.8% to an estimated 9.6%, worth roughly $2.1M in retained ARR.

## Recommendation

Move two engineers onto Workflows onboarding for H2 and make Workflows setup a step in the standard implementation process for every new account.
